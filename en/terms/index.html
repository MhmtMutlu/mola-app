In short

2. Who may use it

  • As a parent or guardian, to set up Mola you must be of legal age in the country you are in and be the parent or legal guardian of the child who uses the device you manage.
  • A child's device is intended to join the family with the QR code the parent shows on their own phone. By connecting your child's device you allow the child to use Mola and accept these terms on the child's behalf as well. You may not connect a device without being the parent or guardian of the child who uses it.

We recommend talking with your child before setup: when you set the rules together, the agreement lasts longer.

5. What Mola does and does not do

  • Mola offers daily limits, school and sleep modes, time requests, a break given by the parent and focus programs.
  • Mola does not read messages, photos or browsing history; it does not track location; it does not show the parent how long the child spent in which app.
  • Mola uses Apple's Screen Time (Family Controls) system. That system belongs to Apple and Apple may change it. Therefore:
    • A rule may be delayed, take effect early or late, or fail to work because of an iOS update or a system fault. "Time left today" is an approximate value.
    • A break given by the parent or a new rule does not reach the child's phone instantly: it is applied when Mola is opened on the child's phone or when iOS gives the app a turn in the background (we do not use notifications on children's phones). The app shows whether the command has been applied on the device. Scheduled rules such as school, sleep and the daily limit are stored on the phone and are not affected by this.
    • If Screen Time access on the child's device was not approved with the Apple account of the parent in Family Sharing, the access can be turned off in Settings and the app can be deleted. The app says so plainly.
  • For these reasons, do not rely on Mola as the only measure ensuring your child's safety.
